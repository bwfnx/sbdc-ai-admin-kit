# The Skill Standard

> Written for Maryland and points at Neoserra — check anything that touches your own CRM, programs, or reporting rules before you lean on it. MIT and unsupported — fork it, change it, don't wait on me.

What every skill should have before an advisor touches it. The five headings are **Joe Burnes's Skill Standard** (Hawaii SBDC). Under each is how Maryland meets it. The two rules at the end are what keeps the staff guide honest.

## 1. Clear name and purpose
- A title a person would say out loud, a one-line tagline, and a short summary.
- The summary says who it's for and what comes back — not how clever it is.

## 2. When to use it — and when not to
- The skill's `description` line **is** the trigger. The platform reads it to decide when to load the skill, so it names the job and the phrases people actually say.
- An explicit "what this will never do" list lives in the skill's body.

## 3. Required inputs
- A "have ready" list: the transcript, the export, the business plan. Name the file type and where to get it (for example, "Neoserra Training Events export, next six months").

## 4. Questions when information is incomplete
- The opening questions are written out in the skill, word for word.
- The skill says what it does if an answer never comes (proceed and mark the gap, or stop).

## 5. Workflow and decision points
- What it does, in order, and where it branches.
- The shape of what comes back: named sections, tables, or files.
- The follow-up menu, if it has one, with the options that always appear.

## The two rules that don't bend
1. **Every trigger phrase in a guide is pulled from the skill file.** Nobody gets to make up a command that sounds good. If a phrase was inferred rather than stated, it's marked "(inferred)" — and there should be almost none.
2. **Every "it will never do X" is a real guardrail in the skill.** Not something we wish were true. If the guide and the skill disagree, the guide is wrong and we fix the guide.

## Scoring checklist

Score any skill, converted or written fresh. Every line is pass/fail.

| # | Check | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has a title, a one-line tagline, and a summary saying who it's for and what comes back | ☐ |
| 2 | `description` names the job and at least two phrases people really say | ☐ |
| 3 | Body has a "never does" list, and every item is enforced by an instruction in the body | ☐ |
| 4 | Has a "have ready" list naming each input and where it comes from | ☐ |
| 5 | Opening questions are written out, with what happens if they go unanswered | ☐ |
| 6 | Workflow is in order, with its branch points named | ☐ |
| 7 | Output shape is named (sections, tables, or files) — not "a helpful response" | ☐ |
| 8 | Lists that must match a system exactly (CRM categories, form fields) are copied, not paraphrased | ☐ |
| 9 | Says what it needs from the platform (web search, a connector, file upload) | ☐ |
| 10 | Has been cold-tested per TESTING.md, and the results are written down | ☐ |
